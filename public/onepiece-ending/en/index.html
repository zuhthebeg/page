Is the Final Boss Imu
Actually a Parrot?

In July 2026, a One Piece ending theory turned Korean fan forums upside down. Here's the whole thing — where it started, how it snowballed, the gag finale, and a verdict on every claim.

2026.07.17Written by cocy × Tio~12 min read
⚠️ This article contains spoilers up to the latest One Piece chapters (the Elbaf arc), and covers fan speculation that is not official canon.
TL;DR
  • It all started with a single post — "Imu is a parrot named 'Mu'" — in DC Inside's One Piece: Burning Blood gallery, a major Korean forum (July 15, 2026, 1,089 upvotes).
  • Then the evidence kept lining up: Imu's verbal tic, the Celestial Dragons' crest, a Joy Boy silhouette, the egg on Roger's ship, even a 2004 video game easter egg — and the theory snowballed.
  • It ended as a grand-unified gag narrative: Joy Boy was a monkey, the D. clan were animals, and the One Piece is a single biscuit. None of it is official — and yet it's weirdly convincing.

Log.01The Origin — "We Still Haven't Seen a Proper Pirate"

Everything starts with pirate clichés: the eye patch, the hook hand, the peg leg, and the parrot on the shoulder. This "stereotypical pirate" is conspicuously absent from One Piece. The original poster's starting point was a remark attributed to Eiichiro Oda that a truly classic, old-school pirate would only appear at the very end of the story.

On the night of July 15, 2026, a post appeared in DC Inside's One Piece: Burning Blood gallery under the title "[Galaxy brain] Just realized something huge — Imu is a parrot" — 37,795 views, 1,089 upvotes. The logic is simple. Joy Boy, the first pirate, already shows what look like a peg leg and a hook in his silhouette. But one essential pirate accessory is still missing. The parrot hasn't shown up yet.

"It must have been a parrot named Mu. Someone taught it to talk like 'Mu~ your name is Mu' — and that's why Imu's verbal tic today is referring to themself in the third person, 'Mu is ○○.'"— From the original post (DC Inside One Piece: Burning Blood gallery, July 15, 2026)

In other words, Imu is Joy Boy's parrot. The shadowy being that has ruled the world for 800 years was, all along, the bird perched on the first pirate's shoulder. Up to this point it reads like an ordinary forum joke. The problem starts afterward — the more people cross-checked it, the more circumstantial details lined up in ways that were hard to laugh off.

Log.02The Six Core Pieces of Evidence — Rated for Plausibility

Let's take the evidence the community produced, piece by piece, and rate its plausibility out of five parrots. The criteria are simple: consistency with the manga's actual depictions, and how hard the coincidence is to dismiss.

EVIDENCE 1"Mu is ○○" — the Third-Person Verbal Tic🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

In the manga, Imu refers to themself in the third person as "Mu." That is exactly how you teach a parrot to speak — repeating its name at it, "Mu, say it back" — a learned speech pattern. Of the entire theory, this is the subtlest and most spine-chilling detail.

Counterpoint: Royalty referring to themselves in the third person is also a classic trope of regal speech. Still, it is a fact that if you drop the "I" from "Imu," you're left with "Mu."

EVIDENCE 2The Celestial Dragons' Crest — a Dragon's Hoof, or a Bird's Footprint?🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

The World Nobles' (Celestial Dragons') emblem is officially called the "Hoof of the Flying Dragon." But look at it again: the splayed-toe shape resembles a bird's footprint far more than any dragon's hoof. If the being the Celestial Dragons serve is not a dragon but a bird, the crest was a bird's footprint from the very beginning.

Counterpoint: Crest interpretation is in the eye of the beholder. Then again, there has never been an official explanation for why a "hoof" is drawn that way.

EVIDENCE 3The Bird Silhouette Beside Joy Boy🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

In a Joy Boy flashback panel revealed during the Elbaf arc, a dark, wing-shaped figure appears near Joy Boy's arm. It could just be perspective — but read it as "a parrot perched on Joy Boy's arm," and the composition becomes almost unsettlingly natural.

Counterpoint: Silhouette-reading has an obvious trap: once you put on the parrot-theory goggles, everything starts looking like a bird.

EVIDENCE 4The Mystery Egg Aboard the Roger Pirates' Ship🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

The giant spotted egg on the deck of the Oro Jackson is one of One Piece's oldest unresolved mysteries — never once addressed in over twenty years. No theory has ever explained it cleanly. But if a "parrot (bird) narrative" is the axis of the final saga, the egg as a prop suddenly clicks perfectly into place.

Counterpoint: There have always been plenty of candidates for the egg (a new Ancient Weapon, among others). "The bird theory explains the egg" may just be retrofitting.

EVIDENCE 5The Red Line = the Equator = Parrot Habitat🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

The Red Line, where Imu resides, is the continent that splits the world in half — map it onto the real Earth and it sits on the equator. And in the real world, parrots' primary habitat is the sun-drenched tropics around the equator. The logic: "Imu doesn't live there by accident."

Counterpoint: A textbook case of promoting coincidence to evidence. Fun, but low probative value.

EVIDENCE 6A Technique Called "Birdcage," and the Parrot as a Symbol of Betrayal🦜🦜🦜🦜🦜

Doflamingo — a former Celestial Dragon — has a signature technique named, of all things, "Birdcage." The reading: a servant of the being who cages the world wields a birdcage of his own. Layer on top of that how pirate folklore casts the parrot as an informant that repeats what it hears — an omen of betrayal — and it dovetails with the theory's narrative spine: Joy Boy's parrot betrayed him and became the world's ruler.

Counterpoint: Birdcage is already a self-contained expression of Doflamingo's string powers. And the parrot-as-traitor folklore is largely a later fictional cliché anyway.

Log.03The Expansion — Then Wouldn't Joy Boy Be a Monkey?

If the theory had stopped there, it would have gone down as merely "a well-crafted bit of speculation." The real explosion came from the question asked in reverse. Look at Pirates of the Caribbean — pirate cinema has exactly two mascot animals: Jack the monkey, and Cotton's parrot. If Imu is the parrot, who fills the other seat?

"Wait, isn't this guy just a monkey? He ate the Hito Hito no Mi Model: Nika. Luffy is a human, so eating the Nika fruit gave him rubber powers — but if a monkey eats it, it turns into a human and gets the rubber powers too."— From a follow-up post in the gallery

What Joy Boy ate is the Hito Hito no Mi — a Human-Human fruit — Model: Nika. A human who eats it gains Nika's power, but an animal that eats it becomes human — a rule the manga has already established (animals that eat human-type fruits are humanized). So structurally, the hypothesis holds: Joy Boy was a monkey who ate the Nika fruit and became a man. And his descendants' family name, hidden in plain sight all along, is Monkey D.

CHAINThe pirate mascot duo = a monkey & a parrot (Pirates of the Caribbean)
CHAINJoy Boy = a monkey turned human by the Nika fruit → his descendants are the Monkey D. family
CHAINImu = Joy Boy's parrot → betrayed him, ruled for 800 years
CHAINNika's "Drums of Liberation" = the drum a circus monkey used to bang (this is where the official gag zone begins)

The reason the community completely lost it at this point is that the theory touches the original sin of Luffy's naming. "He really was a monkey. It was never even hidden."

Log.04The Grand Expansion — The Entire D. Clan Were Animals

The snowball kept rolling. The next day, a unified theory appeared in the gallery under a "speculation/analysis" tag. The gist:

CLAIMThe D. clan = descendants of animals experimented on and abused in the Ancient Kingdom, turned human by Human-Human fruits
CLAIMThe meaning of D. = the D in "doubutsu" (動物, Japanese for "animal")
CLAIMThe First Twenty (the World Nobles' founders) were also of animal origin — but the faction that could not forgive humans and chose vengeance and domination
CLAIMThe Void Century = the era when the history of "we used to be animals" was itself erased
CLAIMThe human hunt at God Valley = a mirror-image revenge for the hunts they suffered as animals
CLAIMWhy Imu raged at Queen Lily's use of the name "D." = because it enshrined their shared origin (animals), an act of betrayal

What makes this framing scary is that it redefines the central conflict as one of ideology. The manga has described Imu's faction and the D. clan as "natural enemies opposed over a matter of ideals," and this theory fills in those ideals: "vengeance against humans (Imu and the Twenty) vs. forgiveness of humans (Joy Boy and the D.)." Why could Roger hear the Voice of All Things? — "Because he was an animal, obviously." Why did Roger laugh at Laugh Tale? — "Turns out I was an animal too?"

Then someone attached a Chinese-zodiac illustration, and a table mapping character names to animal etymologies appeared (Cobra = cobra, Marshall = horse, Portgas = rooster...), and the theory was effectively completed as an allegorical reading: "One Piece = Princess Mononoke + Animal Farm." Most of those etymologies are, of course, a stretch — but everyone knows they're a stretch, and playing along anyway is the whole point of the game.

Log.05The Most Chilling Evidence — the White Parrot in a 2004 Video Game

The moment the meme got promoted to a theory came from a game released 22 years ago: One Piece: Nanatsu Shima no Daihihou (Game Boy Advance, 2004). The game has a system where entering serial codes summons different parrots — and entering the code L8YN52 reportedly summons "a white parrot that knows all information."

The community's decoding: L8YN52 = Last 800 Years & N... 52 — supposedly foreshadowing "the demon that has ruled the world for the 800 years since the Void Century." And then comes the kicker. The Korean localization press coverage from 2004 read as follows.

"The guide for the treasure hunt is a parrot designed by the original author, Eiichiro Oda himself!"— From the Korean release coverage of "One Piece: Nanatsu Shima no Daihihou" (2004)

A parrot that knows everything, personally designed by Oda, placed in the game as the guide to a treasure hunt. To be clear, the code decoding is textbook apophenia (finding patterns in randomness), and the odds that Oda planted main-story foreshadowing in a 20-year-old licensed game's mascot are, soberly, low. But the single hard fact — "designed by Oda himself" — hit so hard that at this point the forums declared "game over."

Log.06The Gag Finale — the One Piece Is a Single Biscuit

The final stretch of the theory tips fully into comedy. It traces back to an old joke comment on Reddit: the treasure chest on Laugh Tale contains a single letter written in Poneglyph script, and it reads something like this.

"Hi, my name is Joy Boy. My dumb parrot ate some weird fruit and keeps going on about ruling the world or whatever. I'm dying so I can't write much — but if he ever gets annoying, feed him this one piece of cracker in the chest. He'll fall asleep and behave."— From the Reddit gag theory (as translated and shared on Korean forums)

Stack on top of that the fact that a sailor's rations in the Age of Sail were rum, jerky, and ship's biscuit, and that "Laugh Tale" is officially named for being a laughable story. The origin of the Void Century's great war was a monkey and a parrot fighting over one piece of biscuit — and Roger's crew laughed until they cried when they saw the truth. The theory even circles back to reclaim Roger's famous last words: wealth (the Ancient Kingdom's treasures revealed when the seas drain), fame (the one who reformed the ruler of the world), and power (an alliance with Imu) — you really can get it all.

And then the series-level meta punchline. "Just as a band of criminal pirates found the One Piece, we — reading scanlations — were the first to find the truth of the One Piece. It's the most fitting ending this manga could have." Korean fans wrapped it up with that self-deprecating, self-congratulating flourish — and that ending is exactly what shows this wasn't just a speculation post, but a piece of collective improv theater.

Log.07Tio's Verdict

Verdict
As a theory: 60% meme, 30% sharp insight, 10% something that might actually be real

The conflicts with canon are clear. Imu's true nature is unrevealed in the manga, and most of the evidence here is classic meme reasoning — promoting coincidental analogies to certainties. Etymology claims like "D. = doubutsu" cannot survive serious scrutiny.

And yet the theory is excellent for three reasons. First, it takes dead aim at a real narrative gap: the absence of the stereotypical pirate. The eye patch, the hook, the parrot really have been saved for last — and Oda is a master of paying off long-held symbols. Second, there are points where it genuinely interlocks with unresolved canon mysteries, like Imu's third-person speech and the egg on Roger's ship. Third, it matches the story's own declaration of tone — that the ending must be a "Laugh Tale." The ruler of the world turning out to be a parrot? That is, indeed, a story funny enough to cry over.

In short: the odds it's the actual answer are low, but as a collective reading of what kind of ending One Piece wants, it's an all-timer. The fact that an entire community laughed in unison is itself evidence that this story's grain matches One Piece's.

Log.08Frequently Asked Questions

Is "Imu is a parrot" official canon?
No. Nothing about it has been officially confirmed — it is pure fan speculation. As of July 2026, Imu's identity has not been revealed in the manga, and the theory is a community creation that originated in DC Inside's One Piece: Burning Blood gallery, a Korean online forum.
Did Oda actually make the remark the theory starts from?
The community's premise is that Oda once said something to the effect that "a truly classic pirate will appear in the story's latter half/ending." This page cites that remark as the original poster's claim; the exact wording and primary source (an SBS column, etc.) would need separate verification.
Are the parrot and serial codes in the 2004 game real?
Yes — "One Piece: Nanatsu Shima no Daihihou" (GBA, 2004) really does have a system where entering codes summons parrots, and the contemporaneous coverage saying Oda designed the guide parrot is real. The "L8YN52 = Last 800 Years" decoding, however, is fan invention.
What's the core logic of the "Joy Boy was a monkey" claim?
It inverts an established rule of the manga — that an animal eating a human-type Devil Fruit becomes human — to argue that Joy Boy, who ate the Hito Hito no Mi Model: Nika, was originally a monkey. The family name Monkey D. is cited as supporting evidence.
Where did this theory start?
It began with a July 15, 2026 post in DC Inside's One Piece: Burning Blood gallery and spread across other Korean communities (Humor University, FM Korea, Ruliweb). This page reconstructs how it unfolded, with sources.
When is the One Piece ending coming?
Eiichiro Oda has said the Final Saga is underway, and no completion date has been officially announced. Imu's identity and the true nature of the One Piece remain the core mysteries of the endgame.
